MIXED CLOSE · 5% TREASURY · JAPAN LINK

THE INDEX LOOKED CALM.
MOST STOCKS DID NOT.

The S&P 500 gained 0.17% and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.39%, while the Dow slipped 0.18%. Those small moves hid a weaker session underneath. Most stocks fell, the 10-year Treasury yield returned to 5%, and semiconductor strength kept the indexes steady. The BOJ also raised rates to 1.25%. That matters to the U.S. because Japan is one of the largest foreign holders of Treasuries, linking Japanese policy to future demand for U.S. debt.

JAPAN HOLDINGS · RELATIVE YIELDS · FX HEDGING

WHY JAPAN MATTERS
TO WALL STREET.

Japanese institutions and official reserves hold a large amount of U.S. government debt. When BOJ policy changes domestic yields, the yen or hedging costs, the relative appeal of Treasuries can change too. Any lasting shift in that demand can reach the U.S. 10-year yield and then the valuation of stocks.

HOLDINGS

JAPAN IS ONE OF THE LARGEST FOREIGN HOLDERS

JGB YIELDS

HOME ASSETS CAN BECOME MORE ATTRACTIVE

Higher Japanese yields can reduce the need to reach abroad for income.

FX HEDGING

THE DOLLAR RETURN IS NOT THE WHOLE RETURN

Currency-hedging costs can change what a Treasury earns in yen terms.

INTERVENTION

YEN PRESSURE ADDS ANOTHER CHANNEL

The choice between guidance, spot action and repo liquidity can change the market spillover.
