SEP 24, 2026

10-YEAR AT 5.225% · IRAN HEADLINE · AI CREDIT SPLIT

THE HEADLINE SAVED THE INDEX.
IT DID NOT FIX THE MARKET.

September 23 put the bond market in control. September 24 pushed that pressure further. Strong jobless claims and hawkish Fed talk lifted the 10-year yield to 5.225%, and stocks opened lower. Then a report said the U.S. and Iran were exploring a phased path to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Stocks snapped back, but oil soon recovered and more stocks still fell than rose. The S&P 500 lost 0.02%, the Dow fell 0.31% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.01%. The close looked calm. The market underneath did not.

META LEADS · CHIPS SPLIT · ORACLE FALLS

AI WAS NOT
ONE TRADE ANYMORE.

Meta gained 4.50%, Alphabet rose 1.34%, AMD added 2.38% and Intel climbed 3.91%. Those moves showed that demand for AI products and computing was still alive. The funding side told a different story. Oracle fell 3.47% as questions around Project Jupiter reached the credit market. The market rewarded companies closest to demand while becoming more careful with capital-heavy projects and their lenders.

WHY IT MATTERS
SEP 24, 2026

AI DEMAND · AI FUNDING · WEAK BREADTH

AI NOW HAS
TWO BALANCE SHEETS.

The first balance sheet belongs to companies turning AI into products, users and revenue. Meta's rally showed that side was alive. The second belongs to the data centers, power systems, loans and private capital needed to build everything. Oracle and Project Jupiter showed where that side can strain. Demand can remain strong while funding and execution get weaker. That split matters more than calling the whole AI trade bullish or bearish.

FOUR THINGS TO KEEP IN MIND
AI DEMAND

PRODUCTS AND PLATFORMS STILL WORK

Meta, Alphabet and selected chips showed that buyers still reward real adoption.

AI FUNDING

CAPITAL IS NO LONGER AUTOMATIC

Power delays, project loans and wider credit spreads can slow the buildout.

AI AGENTS

WINNERS AND LOSERS ARE FORMING

Agents can help consumers while challenging businesses built on search friction and hard-to-cancel services.

REAL ECONOMY

THE INDEX STILL HID THE STRAIN

Small caps, housing and rate-sensitive sectors remained weaker than the headline benchmarks.

RATE SPEED · OIL CONFIRMATION · AI CREDIT · BREADTH

THE NEXT TEST IS
WHETHER THE RESCUE BECOMES REAL.

September 23 showed that bonds could overpower the relief trade. September 24 showed that one headline could still reverse the index. What it did not show was a healthier market. Yields made a new high, oil rejected part of the peace move and breadth stayed weak.

THE BIG PICTURE

The index can survive on headlines and a few strong AI names. A durable rally still needs lower macro pressure, stable credit and broader participation.

A lasting decline would show that the market believes the Hormuz path can work.
