SEP 24, 2026 · LONG YIELDS · HEADLINE REVERSAL · AI FUNDING

THE INDEXES RECOVERED FROM THEIR LOWS.THE MARKET DID NOT HEAL.

September 23 put the bond market in control. September 24 pushed the pressure further. Strong labor data and hawkish Fed commentary lifted long yields, then a reported U.S.-Iran path to reopen Hormuz triggered a sharp equity rebound. The close looked calm, but oil, breadth and AI funding did not confirm a genuine reset.

OPENING PRESSURE

The rates shock extended as resilient data kept tighter policy in play.

HEADLINE RESPONSE

A REPORTED PATH TO REOPEN HORMUZ FORCED A REBOUND

The indexes recovered quickly as traders reduced part of the oil-risk trade.

MARKET VERDICT

RELIEF ARRIVED BEFORE CONFIRMATION

The headline changed positioning. It did not settle the bond, oil or breadth questions.

01

MACRO CONFIRMATION

THE MACRO PRESSURE STAYED INTACT.

Over the full month ending September 24, the 10-year yield rose from 4.70% to 5.18%, the 30-year moved from 5.23% to 5.47%, and front-month WTI climbed from $85.01 to $94.61. A possible diplomatic opening helped equities, but rates and energy did not deliver synchronized relief.

Both Treasury maturities and crude ended above their August 24 closes. The three markets did not deliver synchronized easing.

02

MARKET ROTATION

AI SPLIT INTO TWO TRADES.

Meta, Alphabet, AMD and Intel showed that investors still favored AI platforms and computing leaders. Oracle moved the other way as Project Jupiter brought power, timing and financing risk into view. The tape favored businesses closest to monetization while becoming more selective about capital-heavy buildouts.

ROTATION CLASSIFICATIONAI DEMAND / FUNDING SPLIT

Capital stayed with platforms, selected chips and cash-generating leaders. It moved away from projects where power, permits, leverage and delivery timelines became harder to ignore.

DEMAND WINNERS

PLATFORMS AND SELECTED CHIPS

Meta gained 4.50%, Alphabet rose 1.34%, AMD added 2.38% and Intel climbed 3.91%.

FUNDING TEST

ORACLE BROKE FROM THE GROUP

Oracle fell 3.47% as Project Jupiter shifted attention from AI demand to construction and financing execution.

MARKET HEALTH

SUPPORT REMAINED SELECTIVE

Payments and healthcare offered pockets of strength, but retail, utilities and industrials remained broadly weak.

03

RATES SET THE PRESSURE. HEADLINES SPLIT THE TAPE.

Long rates set the broad pressure, the Iran report reversed positioning and Project Jupiter exposed company-level funding risk. Their reach and time horizons were different, so they should not be read as one trade.
