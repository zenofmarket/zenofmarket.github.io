SEP 14, 2026 · AI FEAR HITS CHIPS · CYBER TAKES THE FLOW

AI FEAR HIT CHIPS.THE MARKET ROTATED.

The 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched 5%, Brent settled at $105.68 and a weekend call to slow AI development punished semiconductor shares. Yet the S&P 500 lost only 0.48%, and more stocks rose than fell. The session was not a retreat from risk. It was a sharp repricing of where AI risk sits and who may benefit from it.

MACRO CONFIRMATION

RATES AND OIL LEFT LITTLE ROOM FOR THE AI SHOCK.

Over the full month ending September 14, the 10-year yield moved from 4.68% to 4.97%, the 30-year moved from 5.25% to 5.34%, and front-month WTI moved from $82.40 to $101.39. The 10-year rose 29 basis points and crude 23.0%, making an AI-specific shock harder for high-duration hardware to absorb.

02

MARKET ROTATION

THE DAMAGE WAS DEEP. IT WAS NOT BROAD.

Nvidia lost 3.36%, Broadcom 4.77%, Micron 5.25% and Marvell 7.32%. At the same time, Palo Alto Networks jumped 13.09%, CrowdStrike 10.85%, Alphabet 3.22% and Microsoft 1.97%. The heatmap shows a decisive transfer from AI hardware risk into security, software, communication services and defensives.

ROTATION CLASSIFICATIONTHE HEATMAP CONFIRMS A SECTOR TRANSFER, NOT A WHOLESALE EXIT FROM EQUITIES.

Nvidia lost 3.36%, Broadcom 4.77%, Micron 5.25% and Marvell 7.32%. At the same time, Palo Alto Networks jumped 13.09%, CrowdStrike 10.85%, Alphabet 3.22% and Microsoft 1.97%. The heatmap shows a decisive transfer from AI hardware risk into security, software, communication services and defensives.

LEADERSHIP

CYBER + SOFTWARE

Palo Alto gained 13.09% and CrowdStrike 10.85%. Microsoft, Palantir and selected software names also rose as the market paid for security exposure.

PARTICIPATION

COMMUNICATIONS + DEFENSIVES

Alphabet, Meta and Netflix advanced while Walmart, Costco, Eli Lilly, AbbVie and UnitedHealth helped keep participation healthier than the index headline.

LAGGARDS

CHIPS + BANKS

Semiconductor losses ran from roughly 3% to 7%. Bank of America fell 5.14%, while Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs lost more than 3%.
