Sovereign yields remained elevated across major markets, and U.S. equities closed lower with the Nasdaq leading the decline. Selling reached growth and cyclical sectors, but bond volatility stayed contained while energy and healthcare advanced. High rates are reaching risk assets. The unresolved question is whether orderly pressure becomes disorder.

Technology, consumer discretionary, industrials and materials were broadly negative, with software, chips and rate-sensitive cyclicals under pressure. Energy and healthcare moved the other way, while Apple stood out as a rare mega-cap winner.

LEADERSHIP

ENERGY + HEALTHCARE

PARTICIPATION

LAGGARDS

GROWTH + CYCLICALS

Amazon, Tesla, Nvidia and AMD fell alongside broad weakness in software, industrials and materials, confirming that selling was not confined to one group.

ROTATION READRISK-OFF WAS BROAD, BUT NOT UNIFORM.

Energy, healthcare and Apple show that capital rotated toward selected defenses and idiosyncratic strength rather than leaving equities indiscriminately.

WHY IT MATTERS
SEP 1, 2026

THE LEVEL OF RATES IS NOT THE SAME AS THE INSTABILITY OF RATES

THE MARKET HAS LEARNED TO LIVE WITH HIGH YIELDS.
THE RISK IS WHEN THEY BECOME DISORDERLY.

Elevated yields have persisted for many months without automatically ending the equity trend. The more important transition is from high-but-orderly rates to fast-and-volatile rates, especially if the U.S. begins repricing independently of the rest of the world.

WHAT THE MARKET IS ALREADY PRICING
ABSOLUTE YIELDS

HIGH IS ABSORBABLE

Markets have continued functioning with elevated rates for an extended period.

TRANSMISSION

VOLATILITY MATTERS MORE

The speed and variability of rate moves determine whether high yields become destabilizing.

MACRO

NOT FAR ENOUGH YET

Jobs, inflation and ISM have generated information without creating a decisive regime shift.
