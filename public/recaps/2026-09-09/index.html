SEP 9, 2026 · TREASURY SUPPORT MISFIRES · BONDS TEST POLICY

THE INDEX HID THE STRESS.BONDS TESTED THE POLICYMAKERS.

All three major indexes closed lower, but the headline loss still understated the weakness beneath the surface. The timing raises a harder question: was Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent preserving ammunition with a measured buyback, or did the restraint show bond sellers that they could keep pressing until policy gave way? The tape cannot prove coercion. The speed of the 10-year yield can show who is winning.

MACRO CONFIRMATION

TREASURY SUPPORT DID NOT ERASE THE OIL SHOCK.

Over the full month ending September 9, the 10-year yield moved from 4.72% to 4.83%, the 30-year moved from 5.25% to 5.28%, and front-month WTI moved from $82.13 to $96.05. Treasury yields were only modestly higher, but crude rose 17.0%, keeping inflation risk prominent despite policy support.

02

MARKET ROTATION

THE MAP WAS RED. THE EXCEPTIONS WERE LOUD.

Broad pressure reached technology, consumer shares, industrials and large parts of healthcare. Memory and selected banks resisted, energy advanced, and Meta gained 6.55% while Alphabet fell 2.28%. The heatmap confirms a low-correlation market in which stock-specific stories still overpowered the common macro factor.

LEADERSHIP

MEMORY + META

Micron gained 2.75%, AMD 3.04% and Intel 1.69%, while Meta surged 6.55%. The strength was concentrated and catalyst-specific rather than a broad technology bid.

PARTICIPATION

BANKS + ENERGY RESISTED

Wells Fargo, Citigroup and several diversified banks held green. Exxon and Chevron also advanced, consistent with an oil backdrop that supported energy while pressuring the wider market.

LAGGARDS

MEGA-CAP + INDUSTRIALS

Alphabet lost 2.28% and Amazon 1.78%, while Nvidia and Broadcom also declined. General Electric, Boeing, homebuilders and much of application software added to the weak breadth.

03

MARKET DRIVERS

TREASURY BROUGHT SUPPORT. BONDS SOLD ANYWAY.

Treasury conducted a reported $6 billion longer-dated buyback, smaller than the prior $8 billion reference but larger than the program's starting level. The long end weakened after the announcement, which kept the contest between policy signaling and bond-market pressure unresolved.
